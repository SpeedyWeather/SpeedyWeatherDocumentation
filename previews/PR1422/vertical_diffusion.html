Vertical diffusion ​

Vertical diffusion represents the turbulent mixing in the vertical of momentum , temperature (via the dry static energy, see below) and specific humidity . In the boundary layer it spreads the surface fluxes of momentum, heat and moisture over the depth of the boundary layer; above, it mixes layers with strong wind shear and removes superadiabatic lapse rates. The diffusivity depends on space and time and is flow-aware: it is recalculated on every time step from the stability of the atmospheric column.

Implementations ​

The following schemes for vertical diffusion are currently implemented

julia
using SpeedyWeather
subtypes(SpeedyWeather.AbstractVerticalDiffusion)
1-element Vector{Any}:
 BulkRichardsonDiffusion

BulkRichardsonDiffusion is the default in both PrimitiveDryModel and PrimitiveWetModel. Its options are passed on as keyword arguments, and vertical_diffusion = nothing disables it

julia
spectral_grid = SpectralGrid(truncation = 32, nlayers = 8)
vertical_diffusion = BulkRichardsonDiffusion(spectral_grid, background_diffusivity = 0.5)
model = PrimitiveWetModel(spectral_grid; vertical_diffusion)
model_without = PrimitiveWetModel(spectral_grid; vertical_diffusion = nothing)

BulkRichardsonDiffusion is explained in the following. It uses the surface drag and wind of the boundary layer (model.boundary_layer, the default) and is called after the surface fluxes. In a model without a boundary layer only its local and background diffusivity act. See Parameterizations for the general parameterization interface these implement.

Implicit time integration ​

The diffusivity near the surface, where layers can be less than 100 m thick, can easily exceed the stability limit of an explicit time step. The system is diagonally dominant so that the solution is unconditionally stable, does not create new extrema and conserves the column integral.

With couple_surface_fluxes = true (default) the source contains the tendencies of the surface fluxes into the lowermost layer, which is called before the vertical diffusion. The surface fluxes are therefore spread over the boundary layer within the time step instead of heating, moistening or decelerating only the lowermost layer.
